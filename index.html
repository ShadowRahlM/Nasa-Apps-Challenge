            What data does this planner use?

            Climate estimates come from NASA POWER for the selected coordinates, covering the previous 10 complete years. The planner identifies the two highest-rainfall non-overlapping three-month windows in that record. These are climate periods, not verified planting seasons. It uses these daily datasets:

            • PRECTOTCORR — corrected precipitation, in mm/day; summed to compare three-month rainfall windows and estimate seasonal rainfall.
            • T2M — air temperature at 2 metres, in °C; averaged by season.
            • T2M_MAX — daily maximum air temperature at 2 metres, in °C; used to count days above 35°C.
            • EVPTRNS — evapotranspiration, in mm/day; summed and compared with rainfall as a broad water indicator.
            • GWETROOT — root-zone soil wetness index; averaged as a modelled indicator, not a field soil test.

            Each location gets its own two wettest non-overlapping three-month windows, which can fall in different calendar months than March–May and September–November. Trend values describe the historical record, not a forecast. NASA POWER is a gridded estimate and may not match conditions on an individual farm.

            Country names and broad regional groupings use the World Bank country API. Place names and coordinates use Open-Meteo Geocoding; choosing a result shows its available region and district hierarchy.

            Language: The app’s original interface is English. Use your browser or device’s “Translate page” feature for other languages; coverage and farming-term accuracy depend on that translation service. Place search requests names in your browser’s language when the geocoder supports it. Farm coordinates and crop plans are not sent to a translation service by this app.

            NASA mapping: the recommendation view displays MODIS Terra true-colour imagery and provides an optional MODIS Terra 16-day NDVI visualization through NASA GIBS. NDVI is a broad vegetation-greenness indicator from approximately 250 m pixels; mixed land cover, clouds, and acquisition timing limit interpretation. This visualization does not identify crops or change the crop ranking. See NASA’s MOD13Q1 product description. NASA Worldview can be used to explore more imagery layers.

            Not currently integrated into crop suitability: MODIS-derived crop/field classification, FAO GAEZ crop suitability, and SRTM elevation/slope. The crop list has six starter crops and is not complete for every region; crop characteristics are not NASA measurements and must be checked with local agronomy guidance.
